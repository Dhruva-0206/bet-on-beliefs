# Oracle policy (implementation gate)

The averaging interval is the entire fixed 600 seconds beginning when both identical pools are seeded. Seed prices are known at that instant. Every swap first integrates the previous reserve price up to the swap timestamp, then records the new reserve price. Permissionless observations integrate the same known previous price. Finalization integrates only to the fixed end, even when called late. Same-timestamp operations have zero elapsed weight. Time cannot move backwards.

Choose exact piecewise-constant carry-forward, with no spot-price clamping: reserves cannot change except through contract methods, and each such method must integrate before changing reserves. An idle interval therefore has a known unchanged reserve price, not missing samples. No trades gives equal averages and rejects. A late first crank uses the recorded seed price, never the later spot. There is no call-count-dependent progression or manufactured historical observation. No external price feed is used. A final-block price change has only its actual remaining duration of influence.

PASS requires its cumulative integral strictly greater than FAIL times 103/100. Equal or exactly 3% rejects. Both integrals cover identical durations, so comparison needs no division by duration. All multiplication, addition and comparisons use checked U256. Prices use floor(tUSD reserve * 10^18 / BELIEF reserve); a zero quantized price is invalid. Invalid arithmetic rejects the operation.

This choice preserves liveness without requiring keepers. It does not defend against sustained manipulation: a trader holding a distorted reserve ratio for a substantial part of the window can change the outcome. Sparse cranks cannot fix or worsen that exposure. Tests must demonstrate both exact integration and this limitation before trading is implemented. This policy assumes complete mediation of every reserve change; any future reserve mutation path must integrate first.

Even a one-second distortion can pass if its price magnitude is sufficiently large. The oracle deliberately makes no manipulation-resistance claim. Tests include both a modest last-second fixture that fails and an extreme last-second fixture that passes. A trading cost or liquidity constraint is not an oracle guarantee.
